# Business And Pilot Hypotheses

This document preserves business-case and pilot ideas as unvalidated planning hypotheses. It is human/planning reference material, not product-design authority and not implementation direction.

Use it only when the conversation turns toward pilot strategy, validation, business model, fundraising, or go-to-market. Current product design authority remains the rest of General context.

## Working Business Hypothesis

FieldPlatform may be commercially plausible as a community-first operating layer for local participation ecosystems, especially where event discovery, community continuity, newcomer entry guidance, venue overlap, and repeated rhythms are fragmented across tools.

This should be treated as a hypothesis, not a proven market fact.

## Beachhead Hypothesis

Possible initial wedge:

- local embodied, relational, movement, meditation, social-practice, and community-care scenes
- participants seeking orientation and appropriate first steps
- facilitators who need context-rich discoverability without ad-targeting dynamics
- community stewards who need entry guidance, continuity, and respectful suggestions
- venues that host overlapping communities

The wedge may be narrow but high-context. That can be useful for learning before broader expansion.

## Monetization Hypotheses

Participant experience should likely remain free during early validation.

Possible future monetized segments:

- facilitators
- community stewards or collectives
- venues or space partners
- native ticketing or registration fees
- setup/migration/onboarding services
- grants, sponsorships, or municipal/cultural partnerships during pilots

Do not let monetization assumptions distort the current product into a generic event marketplace, CRM, ad product, or organizer funnel.

## Pilot Validation Hypothesis

The pilot question should not be "can we get downloads?" The stronger question is:

Does better orientation produce more meaningful participation?

Possible validation signals:

- communities publish enough real upcoming offerings to make the product useful
- community pages include clear entry guidance
- participants follow communities or save events after understanding why they appear
- event detail views lead to appropriate attendance or interest
- first attendance leads to second attendance or continued community relationship
- people move across adjacent communities with retained context and trust
- organizers/stewards keep publishing because the platform reduces fragmentation

## Risks To Track

- The product becomes a generic event listing.
- The wedge is too narrow without a broader expansion path.
- Community tools drift toward CRM or surveillance.
- Event creation starts to feel like marketing or ad targeting.
- Suggestions feel like tagging, claiming, or ownership.
- Generated patterns are mistaken for stewarded communities.
- Business pressure pushes premature monetization onto participants.
- Trust and privacy expectations are underestimated.

## Current Status

These are hypotheses for later planning and validation. Do not treat old generated market numbers, competitor comparisons, or revenue projections as current facts unless they are freshly researched and explicitly adopted in a future task.
