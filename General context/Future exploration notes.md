# Future Exploration Notes

This document preserves promising but not-currently-binding ideas. These notes can inspire advisor/planning work, future mockups, or research, but they are not active implementation direction unless a future prompt or General context update adopts them.

Current product priority remains object surfaces, FieldRelation-powered context, role-gated stewardship, grouped ways in, ordinary language, and the Platform data layer as shared model.

## Orientation Home / Windows

Older generated material repeatedly pointed toward an orientation-first home that helps a person choose how to relate with the field today.

Potential modes:

- Continue what already supports you.
- Explore adjacent worlds.
- Explore the rhythm of the field.
- Deepen what already matters.
- Create, share, or nurture.
- Make space.

Useful principle:

- A home surface should offer concrete windows into participation, not a dense feed or abstract feature menu.
- Each window should show real, recognizable content where possible: known communities, next events, familiar venues, suggested adjacent openings, or current commitments.
- The experience should invite choice without implying that the app knows what the user should do.

Current status:

- Exploratory. Do not build a full home/windows system until the current object-page and relation-context questions are clearer, or until a prompt explicitly frames home/orientation as the next pressure test.

## Map And Rhythm Exploration

Older generated material explored map and rhythm concepts in depth. These remain promising, but they should not override the current object-surface and FieldRelation work.

Potential map modes:

- Live map: what concrete opportunities are happening soon.
- Rhythm of the field: how communities, venues, and practices move through time.
- Familiar territory: where the user already has relationship.
- Adjacent worlds: what is connected to what the user already knows.
- Wider field: how scenes connect across cities or regions.

Potential visual ontology:

- Events are bounded.
- Communities are ambient.
- Venues are anchored.
- Adjacency is motion through time.

Useful principle:

- Maps should orient rather than overwhelm.
- The map can reveal place, activity, rhythm, community presence, and connection.
- Panels, tooltips, and object pages should explain details and actions.
- Rhythm views must bridge from pattern into concrete doors: next events, communities, facilitators, venues, or ways in.

Current status:

- Exploratory. A future map mockup should answer one narrow question, such as "How does a public event page link into nearby community rhythm?" or "How should an adjacent-world trace explain why two contexts are connected?"

## Validation And Usability Testing

Useful older guidance:

- Test whether users understand what they are looking at without needing internal vocabulary.
- Watch for cognitive load, unclear actions, role confusion, and whether the surface feels like orientation or metadata.
- Use task-based sessions with participants, facilitators, and stewards.
- Ask whether community stewards feel supported without feeling watched or turned into CRM operators.
- Check whether recommendations and connections explain why they appear.
- Track meaningful participation, not just clicks.

Potential validation signals:

- A user can identify what an object is and what action is available.
- A user understands why an event/community/venue appears.
- A user can find a first step without interpreting domain terms.
- A steward can understand what accepting/refining/declining a suggestion changes.
- Participants do not feel pushed toward more participation or deeper participation by default.

Current status:

- Active as process guidance, but concrete metrics are unvalidated until a real pilot or user-testing plan exists.

## Accessibility, Ethics, And Privacy

Useful older guidance:

- Prefer recognition over recall.
- Use progressive disclosure to manage complexity.
- Provide clear feedback for actions such as following, saving, suggesting, accepting, or requesting access.
- Do not rely on color alone; use labels, shapes, icons, or patterns where meaning matters.
- Keep important controls keyboard-accessible.
- Use motion sparingly and let users reduce or pause animation.
- Collect only data needed for the experience being offered.
- Explain why recommendations and connections appear.
- Avoid dark patterns, urgency tricks, identity-hardening personalization, and nudges toward more participation.

Current status:

- Aligned with current product guardrails. Apply these principles when designing actual surfaces, but do not import generated implementation specifics unless a surface demands them.

## Ask Before Joining / Human Context Paths

Future expansion: users who are interested but uncertain may need a low-pressure human route before joining, attending, or requesting access.

Possible paths:

- Ask facilitator.
- Ask a community steward.
- Ask before joining.
- Ask in a beginner or interested-participant space.

This supports trust, threshold, and context uncertainty without requiring the user to interpret the relation model. Overlapping community chats, forums, and beginner/interested channels are interesting possibilities, but they should not become the active design focus until object surfaces, semantic separation, and relation-powered context are clearer.
