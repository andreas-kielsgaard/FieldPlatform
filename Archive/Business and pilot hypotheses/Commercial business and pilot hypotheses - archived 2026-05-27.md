# Business And Pilot Hypotheses

## Planning Reference: Business And Pilot Hypotheses

This document collects early business, pilot, and validation hypotheses. It is not product authority and it is not evidence that the market has been validated.

The working business idea is that FieldPlatform could become a community-first operating layer for local participation ecosystems where events, community continuity, entry guidance, venues, and repeated rhythms are fragmented across tools.

Possible early audiences include:

- local embodied, relational, movement, meditation, social-practice, and community-care scenes
- participants looking for orientation and appropriate first steps
- facilitators who need context-rich discovery without ad-targeting dynamics
- community stewards who need respectful entry guidance and suggestion review
- venues that host overlapping communities

The most important validation question is not "can we get downloads?" It is: does better orientation produce more meaningful participation?

A possible living-field pilot could test whether lightweight needs, offers, care tasks, arrival prompts, and digestion flows help a bounded group become more self-resourcing without replacing direct human interaction.

The main business risk is drift: the product could become a generic event listing, CRM, ad product, organizer funnel, resource catalogue, or app layer that makes living community life more administrative.

## Working Business Hypothesis

FieldPlatform may be commercially plausible as a community-first operating layer for local participation ecosystems, especially where event discovery, community continuity, newcomer entry guidance, venue overlap, and repeated rhythms are fragmented across tools.

This should be treated as a hypothesis, not a proven market fact.

## Beachhead Hypothesis

Possible initial wedge:

- local embodied, relational, movement, meditation, social-practice, and community-care scenes
- participants seeking orientation and appropriate first steps
- facilitators who need context-rich discoverability without ad-targeting dynamics
- community stewards who need entry guidance, continuity, and respectful suggestions
- venues that host overlapping communities

The wedge may be narrow but high-context. That can be useful for learning before broader expansion.

## Monetization Hypotheses

Participant experience should likely remain free during early validation.

Possible future monetized segments:

- facilitators
- community stewards or collectives
- venues or space partners
- native ticketing or registration fees
- setup/migration/onboarding services
- grants, sponsorships, or municipal/cultural partnerships during pilots

Do not let monetization assumptions distort the current product into a generic event marketplace, CRM, ad product, or organizer funnel.

## Pilot Validation Hypothesis

The pilot question should not be "can we get downloads?" The stronger question is:

Does better orientation produce more meaningful participation?

Possible validation signals:

- communities publish enough real upcoming offerings to make the product useful
- community pages include clear entry guidance
- participants follow communities or save events after understanding why they appear
- event detail views lead to appropriate attendance or interest
- first attendance leads to second attendance or continued community relationship
- people move across adjacent communities with retained context and trust
- organizers/stewards keep publishing because the platform reduces fragmentation

## Living Field Pilot Hypothesis

Living houses, creative residencies, temporary communities, aliveness-oriented day spaces, and other high-trust shared spaces may be useful learning contexts because the field is bounded, proximity is high, and the platform can test whether lightweight self-resourcing and digestion flows create value without replacing direct human interaction.

Possible validation question:

Does a low-friction way to notice and optionally share current needs, directions, offers, care tasks, and discoveries make the group more self-resourcing without requiring a heavy digital catalogue?

Potential validation signals:

- people use arrival prompts to find support, rest, focus, or direct action
- asks and offers lead to human responses that would otherwise have stayed hidden
- care for the space becomes more visible without becoming chore scoring
- useful nuggets are carried forward while most daily signals expire
- participants feel more oriented and less pulled away from the living field
- the tool supports spring/fall transitions and stays quiet during summer/winter modes

This is an unvalidated pilot hypothesis, not product authority.

## Risks To Track

- The product becomes a generic event listing.
- The wedge is too narrow without a broader expansion path.
- Community tools drift toward CRM or surveillance.
- Event creation starts to feel like marketing or ad targeting.
- Suggestions feel like tagging, claiming, or ownership.
- Generated patterns are mistaken for stewarded communities.
- Business pressure pushes premature monetization onto participants.
- Trust and privacy expectations are underestimated.
- A living-space pilot turns proximity, meals, walks, and direct conversation into unnecessary app interactions.
- The app asks for resource catalogues instead of serving situated needs, offers, and thresholds.
