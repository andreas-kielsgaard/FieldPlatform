# The Conscious Tribe

The concept of The Conscious Tribe is an idea of how we can find that feeling of being safe in the world, that once came from knowing that we belonged to a certain tribe of people. The complexity of our lives have changed drastically, and continues to change, as the philosophical, political, economic, and cultural landscape around us changes.   
I wish for conscious tribal living to be the root for creating a global ecosystem of connection, love, and shared nourishment.   
I wish for conscious tribal living to provide an alternative source of safety to the methods of resource hoarding and warring that has marked the initial stages of human consciousness.  
I wish for conscious tribal living to show how and why boundaries are meant to connect rather than separate. 

### Questions

How do modern humans relate?  
How did tribes relate? 

We organize ourselves in groups, and hierarchies, but are not conscious of them.

Who do we hold close to us? What does it mean for us that these people are close to us?  
Which responsibilities do we accept, which do we reject? What are the boundaries of our relations \- what are the interfaces?

# The Structure of My Tribe

I spend most of my time with a few people. I share my heart with them. I live alongside them. They see and hold even the most vulnerable of my pieces. Our souls often vibrate to the same frequency.  
These people are the roots of my tribe.

A small group of people feel like “my people”. I feel that I share a worldview with these people, and I gladly give some of what is mine to help them. It seems like these people think like me \- feel like me. Our souls vibrate to a similar frequency.  
These are the grove of my tribe.

A larger group of people are people I know I can trust. I know that we share at least some common ground, and I appreciate when we explore that together. I am loyal to them to some extent. We share views of the world, and I appreciate them for the way they move through the world. Our souls may vibrate at different frequencies, but there is harmony between us.   
These are the forest of my tribe.

These 3 groups I would consider to be “my tribe”. The people I live alongside, the people that resonate deeply with me, and the people I have appreciation for. They are selected through resonance, and experienced through attachment. If I live a healthy life, those closest to me will be the ones who spend the most time with me.  
All of these people I have some level of spiritual intimacy with. I consider us to be “the same” in some way.   
In a traditional tribe, the roots would be those individuals I share a deep, chosen, personal and equal  bond with. Close trusted friends, life partners.  
The grove would be those that I give freely to without needing anything in return. Family and friends.  
The forest would be those that we appreciate for specific qualities. Other people that live in the same place as me, tradesmen I respect, someone who I particularly enjoy sharing a dance with.

There are also many people who I interact with occasionally, and whom I share experiences and time with to varying degrees. This group of people I am friendly with, but not spiritually intimate with. I may enjoy their being in moments, but I do not consider them to be “one with me” in any way. I do not necessarily share the views that are closest to me with them, but I appreciate the way they are in the world, and enjoy spending time with them in the right setting.   
There may not always be harmony between us, but I appreciate the melody that they play.   
These are friends of my tribe.

Then, there is everyone else. Those whom I have little to no connection with. These are strangers to my tribe. In the modern world I encounter strangers every day. This can be an exciting opportunity, but if I am not sure of who to turn to when strangers turn scary, I will not feel safe to meet those who I don’t know.

## The tribe is an individual thing

My tribe is my tribe alone. Although many members of the trees whom I share roots with, are likely to stand in the same grove, and although those in my grove likely share the same forest, the members are not identical. Especially in modern times these groups can be fractured and form a confusing web. My mother may be a stranger to my closest friend, and the people I dance with, may never meet those whom I sing with. Those who dance with me, do not always dance with each other. My tribe is an individual thing.  
To those who are unconscious of their tribe this feels like a dangerous thing. If my roots suddenly lose their grip on a friend, who will I then share my nutrients with now? Who will help me when I thirst? How can it be, that the person I can no longer sing the same song with, still seems to have a place in my forest?  
Those who are conscious of their tribe know that these things shift, and know that it is not loving to hold on to the roots of someone who is singing a different song. They know that they have the ability to go into the world and find someone new to sing with, and that they still belong in their forest, even if their closest friend is not so close anymore. They know where their different boundaries serve them best. 

## The tribe is a dynamic thing

When I am conscious I see that my tribe is mine to prune and maintain. I am a shifting being, and as I go through life, the melodies that I sing will change. I will make an effort to share the new songs that I learn with those who are close with me, and hope that they will sing them with me. If they know they should be dancing a different dance than mine, I will take a step back and marvel from afar, rather than smother them with my persistence. If their songs change in a different direction there is no harm. A tribal relation is not one of ownership. It is one of belonging. Where I belong, and with whom I belong, changes as I change, and as those around me change. My tribe is a dynamic thing.   
As a conscious tribal being I am aware of this. I am always curious to share my song with friends and strangers, and excited to see my friends pull away into a different dance. Perhaps I will discover a harmony I hadn’t heard before, or witness a dance I don’t yet know how to do. What a wonderful celebration this will be.

# Nurturing the Tribe

The tribe is a relationship, and the nutrient of relating is the meeting. Without the meeting there can be no consciousness, and without consciousness the relations will wither and die, as we fumble in blindness for sustenance.

The tribe is an individual thing, but we are not meant to nurture it by ourselves. There are many things to be conscious of in this existence, and many fears that make us forget, so it is no wonder that often things drift apart even if we do not mean for them to do so.   
Meetings are the nourishment of the tribe, and if we have to dig out every seed of the ground with our bare hands, there will not be much to go around. A healthy tribe requires a healthy tribal ecosystem.

## The Tribal Ecosystem

The tribe is an individual thing, and as such it is a foolish thing to try and build a structure which fits your tribe perfectly. Each branch of your tribe has a different shape, and grows in a different direction than your own, in an endless open-ended pattern. Unlike tribal times, we now live in massive societies and interact with thousands of people throughout our lives. The symphony requires something more dynamic than a structure. Something as living as our world. It requires a forest.

# The Tribe is a Forest

Some of you are connected to me through my roots. We share all the nutrients we have directly with each other. If you thirst or suffer, I share what I have to spare with you. If you are hurt, I lend my nutrients to you for your healing. If a storm tries to blow you away, I hold on to you to the ground.   
My roots are intertwined with yours. You are my root tribe. Your roots are intertwined with others in turn, and that is my benefit, for when you run out of water to share with me, they will lend you more in turn. 

Some of you are standing in the same grove as me. Our fruits feed the same spirits, and our leaves share the same light. Together we offer shelter for those who seek refuge in our midst. We are not intertwined, but we do stand together.   
You may have roots in other groves as well, and that is my benefit. The spirits who live there knows your roots, and often visits the grove we share as well. The light you feed on, nurtures me in turn. 

Some of you are from the same part of forest that I am. I have sung with you, danced among your roots, and slept under your branches. We do not always share the same light, or see the same spirits, but I know that whenever I feel a draw towards the world, I can step among your trees and know that I am safe. When I visit your grove, I sing and dance in your light. When I need a break, I know I can find a soft spot amongst your roots.  
You know other parts of the forest than I do, and that is my benefit. When I wish for new inspiration, you grab my hand and take me on a journey to lands yet unfamiliar. When the night is warm you fill our forest with the dance and song of strangers.

Some of you, I see without knowing. You are strangers to me. I have seen you nearby when my forest friends have invited you to our camp, or when I have journeyed into the world, but I have not yet found my place amongst your roots. Perhaps we shared a song or a dance when we met, or perhaps I only looked at you from a distance as you laughed with my friends. I am curious to know you. Eager to find another place in the world where I am safe. Hopeful that I will feel the warmth of your spirit as you rest at my roots. Wondering how the light of your grove will feel on my leaves. Excited about the shape yours will take when you feel the light of mine. Oh the joy I would feel if our roots could find together even if just for a moment.   
Are you curious about me? Do you wish to love also? You are strange in other ways than I am, and that is my benefit. When I see you in the distance, I get to feel the joy of wanting to share. The excitement of learning. The hope of finding another love in a world that is still new to me.

Some of you, I have not yet met. I might have seen you far away one of those times where I climbed to the very top of my tree, to try and get a glimpse of what else lies out there in the world. Or perhaps I saw a glimpse of you out of the corner of my eye one of those times where I wandered by myself amongst the trees I did not know. Perhaps I do not even know that your tree stands on this earth.   
And yet, even if you are by yourself on a lonely mountain top, even if you only know those who share your oasis in the desert, even if you are holding on with only a single root, waiting to be swept away by the wind. Even then I know that we breathe the same air. We feed the same spirits. We are part of the same circle of life, the two of us. I will share my water with you, and you yours with me. Your being is to my benefit, and mine to yours. Make a nest for the bird that visits your mountain top, and come summer, I will enjoy its song. Hold the water for the camel to drink, and I will marvel when it passes by. Fly away with the wind if that is what you must, for even if you break as you land, I will enjoy the poem that life scrawled on your trunk. 

Be life. Be yourself. I will love you when we meet.

# My Grove; A Radical Ask

A healthy grove stands free to let the light in. It has space for song and dance. It holds a warm fire where residents and visitors can meet, and forest paths through which strangers and friends alike can come to visit.   
There are not many such groves in our world today. Many have covered the sky such that little light can enter, and fear to dance and sing even where there is space to do so. Then when the strangulation becomes too much we uproot and flee to a place so far away that it stands apart from the rest of the forest. A grove with a single tree holds little warmth.

I wish to make a healthy grove for myself. I wish for my grove to be a meeting point for strangers that wish to find love for themselves. I wish for my grove to be an inspiration for others to also nourish theirs.   
My tribe is a dynamic thing, and yet I wish to build a structure that it can grow on, and on which others’ can grow as well. I wish to live in a collective of people who I know to be my tribe. People whom I share roots with, who stand in the same grove as me, who’s trees are part of my forest. I wish for these people to be people I can sing in harmony with. I wish for these people to also feel deeply in their roots the frequency that resonates deeply within me: The desire to be an inspiration for self-love. I wish for them to share this vision of the conscious tribe as a path to self love. 

I see a vision of this collective. There is a private space for each of us where we each plant our own tree. A space that is meant only for ourselves and those we wish to share roots with. A space for our private tribe.  
There are shared spaces which are the edge of our grove. Spaces that nurture us as a community so that we can nourish the space that is our grove. Spaces that are open to those who are friends of our tribe. Our local tribe.  
And then there are the most beautiful space of all, those for our global tribe. The space where those who are waking up to self-love can come and be met. The space where friends can invite strangers, and strangers can become friends. The space which is the center of our vision; the center of how we inspire others to become part of a global conscious tribe. The place that inspires love. Every day, this space is held by one person from this collective. One person from this network of roots. Perhaps there is a campfire (talking space) that is alive every night. Perhaps there is a dance space that is facilitated every night. Perhaps there is a music space that is facilitated every night. There are many ways to meet, and the promise of our collective, is that we will always have a fire that you can come to sit by, where you can sing with us, and dance a dance with us.   
Noone should have to walk through the world without knowing when they will next find a hot campfire with some friendly faces. A place where they can be themselves. A place where the boundaries are clear and they do not have to wander the world in confusion. I wish for my grove to be a place for weary travelers and friends alike. A place of meeting. A place of love.

This I ask of life. I am life, and I will make it so.
